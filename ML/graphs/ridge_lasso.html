Ridge regression and the lasso on the Credit data

The same 400 observations and 11 predictors used for subset selection, now fitted with a penalty on the coefficients instead of a search over subsets. The knob is the shrinkage ratio, that is how much of the least squares fit is left: 1 is least squares, 0 is the null model. Each method is fitted at the λ that produces that ratio, so ridge and the lasso can be read side by side (λ itself is not comparable across the two).

  1. Drag the shrinkage ratio from right to left. The first panel is the eleven coefficients at that budget, with the least squares value behind each bar as a grey tick.
  2. Ridge shrinks all eleven together and never reaches zero, the lasso drops them one at a time. Hover a bar or a curve to follow one predictor across both panels.
  3. The geometry panel takes two predictors at a time, so the constraint region can be drawn. Start on the uncorrelated pair, then switch to Limit and Rating (r = 0.997) and watch the contours collapse onto a line.

Limit and Rating have a correlation of 0.997, so on that pair the RSS contours are an extremely long thin ellipse: least squares can trade one predictor off against the other almost freely, which is what makes its estimates so unstable (β on Limit is +440 and β on Income is −275 in the full fit, both far larger than the effects they are measuring). Ridge pulls the pair towards the origin together and keeps both, the lasso picks one and drops the other. Neither is right in general, and the cross-validation panel is what answers which one predicts better here.
