Ridge regression and the lasso on the Credit data

The same 400 observations and 11 predictors used for subset selection, now fitted with a penalty on the coefficients instead of a search over subsets. One knob, λ, moves the fit from least squares to the null model. Ridge shrinks everything smoothly; the lasso sets coefficients exactly to zero.

  1. Pick ridge or the lasso, then drag λ. Watch the coefficient paths and the count of non-zero coefficients underneath.
  2. The third panel is the same problem with two predictors only, so the constraint region can be drawn. The diamond has corners on the axes, the circle has none.
  3. The last panel is ten-fold cross-validation. The cross is the λ with the lowest CV error, which is the one you would actually use.

Limit and Rating have a correlation of 0.997, so the RSS contours in the third panel are an extremely long thin ellipse: least squares can trade one predictor off against the other almost freely, which is what makes its estimates so unstable. Ridge pulls the pair towards the origin together, the lasso picks one of them and drops the other. Neither is right in general, and which one predicts better on the Credit data is what the cross-validation panel answers.
