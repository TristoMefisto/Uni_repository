Collinearity: two predictors that carry the same information

ISLR Figures 3.14 and 3.15, Credit data (400 customers). Balance is regressed on Limit together with Age, then on Limit together with Rating. Limit and Rating are almost the same variable, and the RSS surface tells you what that does to the coefficients.

  1. Top scatters: Age has nothing to do with Limit; Rating is almost the same variable as Limit.
  2. Bottom: RSS contours around the least-squares point. Round = each coefficient is pinned down; a long valley = many pairs fit equally well.
  3. Press Walk along the valley: the two coefficients swing a lot while RSS barely moves. That is why their SEs explode.

Contours are RSS = RSSmin × (1 + c) for c = 0.5%, 1%, 2%, 5%, 10%, with the intercept profiled out. The elongated valley is the geometry behind the variance inflation factor: VIF = 1/(1 − R²) of one predictor on the other, and the standard error of a coefficient grows with √VIF. In model 2 the t-statistic of Limit collapses from 34.5 to 0.4 while the fit (RSS) is essentially the same as in a regression on Rating alone.
