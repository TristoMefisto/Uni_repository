The Bayes classifier and K-nearest neighbours

ISLR Figures 2.13 to 2.17. Two classes in two dimensions, generated from a known distribution, so the true conditional probability Pr(orange | X) is available. The Bayes classifier uses it and is unbeatable; KNN estimates it from the K closest training points, and K sets its flexibility.

  1. Left: the true probability of orange at every point, and its 50% line: the Bayes boundary, the best any classifier can do.
  2. Middle: KNN votes among the K nearest training points. Move K: small K follows every point, large K smooths towards a line.
  3. Right: training error reaches zero at K = 1; test error is U-shaped and never beats the dashed Bayes rate.

Each class is a mixture of three Gaussian bumps; Pr(orange | x) is the orange density divided by the sum of the two densities, and the Bayes error rate is the integral of the smaller of the two densities. Widen the overlap and both the Bayes rate and the best achievable KNN error rise: this is the classification analogue of the irreducible error.
