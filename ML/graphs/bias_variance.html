The bias-variance trade-off, seen over many training sets

ISLR Figure 2.12 made explicit. Draw R training samples from the same Y = f(X) + ε, fit the same polynomial to each, and look at all the fitted curves together. Their average misses f by the bias; their spread around that average is the variance. Expected test MSE = Var(f̂) + Bias² + σ².

  1. Every thin line is the same model fitted to a different training sample of 50 points.
  2. Thick line: their average. Its gap from the black f is the bias; the fan of thin lines around it is the variance.
  3. Press Sweep flexibility: bias falls, variance rises. The red sum is the expected test MSE; pick the degree at its minimum.

A rigid model (degree 1) gives almost the same line whatever the sample: tiny variance, but the line cannot follow f, so large bias. A very flexible one passes close to f on average but each single fit wanders with its sample: small bias, large variance. Neither extreme minimises the sum. Switch preset: with a near-linear f the bias is already gone at degree 1 or 2, with a highly non-linear f it stays large until high degrees.
