Principal components regression and partial least squares on the Credit data

Both methods fit least squares on M linear combinations of the predictors instead of on the predictors themselves. What separates them is how those combinations are chosen: PCR takes the principal components, which are built from the predictors alone, and PLS builds its own directions using Balance from the first step. The principal components page covers what a component is and where it comes from; this one is about what happens when the response gets a say.

  1. Turn the direction with the angle slider. The blue curve in the second panel is the variance of the projected scores and its peak is the first principal component, which is what PCR would take. The purple curve is how much those same scores covary with Balance, and its peak is where PLS goes instead.
  2. The two peaks are not at the same angle, and that gap is the whole difference between the two methods. Press the two snap buttons to put the scatter on each of them in turn.
  3. Move M in the last two panels. The scree bars are the variance each component carries, the purple dots how strongly it is related to Balance, and the last panel is what that mismatch does to the cross-validation error.

PCA never looks at Balance: the directions come from the predictors alone, so PCR works only when the directions that explain the predictors are also the ones that explain the response. On the Credit data that assumption fails in a way the third panel makes visible. Balance goes up with Limit and down with Income, so the useful direction is the contrast between the two, and a contrast between correlated predictors is exactly a low variance direction (Limit and Rating correlate at 0.997, which is why the eleventh component carries no variance at all). PCR meets that direction only at M = 10, one short of least squares. PLS weights each predictor by its own simple regression coefficient on Balance, so it finds the contrast in three components. On this data set neither beats least squares by much, because n is 400 and p is 11: dimension reduction pays when p is large relative to n.
