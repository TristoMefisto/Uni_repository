Principal components on the Credit data

A principal component is the direction along which the observations vary the most, chosen from the predictors alone without ever looking at the response. This page builds one by hand on two predictors, then rotates a cloud of three to show what "the components explain less and less" looks like, then counts the components of all eleven. What to do with them afterwards is on the PCR and PLS page.

  1. Turn the direction with the angle slider. The crosses are the projections of each observation onto it, and the second panel is the variance of those projections against the angle. Its peak is the first principal component and its trough, 90 degrees away, is the second.
  2. Drag the third panel to rotate three standardized predictors in space. Income, Limit and Rating look like a cloud until you turn it, and then it is a sheet.
  3. Press "Look down the third component". The sheet collapses to a flat ellipse, because that direction carries 0.1 per cent of the variance: three predictors are really carrying two dimensions.

The normalisation of the loadings is what makes the problem well posed: without it the variance could be made arbitrarily large by scaling the coefficients up. Standardizing is a separate choice, and switching it off in the first panel shows why it is usually recommended, since the component then follows whichever variable is measured in the larger units rather than whichever one carries the most information. The three predictors in the middle panel are an extreme case of something that happens whenever predictors are correlated: the cloud occupies fewer dimensions than it has variables, and the components are just a set of axes lined up with the shape it actually has.
