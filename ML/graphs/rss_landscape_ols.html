Least squares as a landscape

Sales regressed on TV advertising, 200 markets (ISLR Advertising data). Every (β₀, β₁) pair is a candidate line; RSS scores how badly it fits. Move the line and watch where it sits on the RSS surface.

  1. Move the two sliders: the navy line moves and the grey segments (residuals) change length.
  2. RSS is the sum of the squared residuals. The contour map and the surface show RSS for every possible line.
  3. Press Jump to OLS: the bottom of the bowl, where no other line does better.

Try the two animations and compare the RSS readout. Pivoting keeps the line through (x̄, ȳ), so it moves along the tilted valley and RSS rises slowly. Shifting moves the whole line away from the data centre, so RSS rises faster.

The pivot line is β₀ = ȳ − β₁·x̄: for any slope, it gives the best intercept. Its negative slope (−x̄ ≈ −147) is why the ellipses tilt down.

At the minimum, β̂₀ = 7.03 and β̂₁ = 0.0475: an extra $1,000 of TV advertising is associated with about 47.5 more units sold. RSE is the typical size of a residual and R² the share of the variance of sales the line explains; both are computed for whatever line you are holding.
