Estimating the test error: validation set, LOOCV and k-fold CV

ISLR Figures 5.2 and 5.4 on the Auto data (n = 392): mpg regressed on polynomials of horsepower, degree 1 to 10. Each method estimates the test MSE of every degree without a separate test set. The point is how much each estimate moves when the random split changes, and how many fits it costs.

  1. Pick a method. In the scatter, grey points train the model and red points are held out; the curve is the fit of the chosen degree on the grey points.
  2. Press New random split: the validation-set curves jump up and down, the k-fold curves barely move, LOOCV never moves (no randomness).
  3. Read the minimum of each curve: every method says degree 2 is much better than degree 1, and that higher degrees add little.

The validation set approach uses only half the data to fit, so its curves sit above LOOCV (the error of a model fitted on fewer points is overestimated) and they move a lot with the split. LOOCV uses n - 1 points for each fit and has no randomness, but it needs n fits (here the leverage shortcut gives the same number from a single fit). k-fold with k = 5 or 10 is close to LOOCV at a fraction of the cost, and repeating it with new folds changes little.
