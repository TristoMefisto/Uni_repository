KNN regression against linear regression, and the curse of dimensionality

ISLR Figures 3.17 to 3.20. KNN regression predicts at x₀ with the average response of the K closest training points: no functional form at all. It wins when the truth is curved and loses when the truth is a line. Add predictors that carry no information and it loses badly, whatever the truth.

  1. Orange step curve: the KNN prediction, the average of the K nearest y values. Navy: the straight-line fit.
  2. Middle: test MSE for every K. The dashed line is the linear regression, which does not depend on K.
  3. Press Sweep p: add predictors that carry no information. KNN gets worse and worse; the line does not.

In p dimensions the K nearest neighbours of x₀ come from a ball whose radius grows quickly with p for a fixed n: a non-parametric method needs many more observations per extra dimension, a parametric one only one more coefficient. That is the price of not assuming a form for f.
