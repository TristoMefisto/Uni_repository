Subset selection on the Credit data

ISLR Figures 6.1 to 6.3 and Table 6.1. Balance regressed on subsets of 11 variables (10 predictors, region split in two dummies), n = 400. All 2,048 least squares models are fitted here, so best subset, forward and backward stepwise selection can be compared, and the size of the final model chosen with Cp, BIC, adjusted R² or 10-fold cross-validation.

  1. Move the model size: the table shows the variables picked by best subset, forward and backward selection at that size (yellow where they disagree).
  2. The first panel shows every model: RSS always falls with size, so it cannot choose between sizes. The criteria panel penalises size and has a minimum.
  3. Cross-validation estimates the test error directly; the one-standard-error rule picks the smallest model within one SE of the lowest point. Reshuffle the folds to see the curve move.

Best subset selection needs 211 = 2,048 fits here; forward and backward selection need 1 + 11·12/2 = 67 each. On these data forward selection keeps rating once it is in, so at four variables it cannot reach the best model (cards, income, student, limit), which drops rating for limit and cards. Beyond three or four variables all the curves are nearly flat: the extra variables reduce the training RSS a little and the test error not at all.
