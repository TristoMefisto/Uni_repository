Residual plots: what a fit is hiding

ISLR Figures 3.9 and 3.11 to 3.13. Auto data, 392 cars: miles per gallon against horsepower. A residual plot puts the residuals against the fitted values; any pattern left in it says the model is missing something. Leverage and studentized residuals tell apart unusual x from unusual y.

  1. Choose linear or quadratic. The middle panel plots residuals against fitted values: a curved red smooth means the model missed a curve.
  2. Tick drag mode and move a point. The dashed line shows the fit without it.
  3. Right panel: far to the right = unusual x (leverage), far up or down = unusual y (outlier). Both at once is the dangerous corner.

Studentized residual: the residual divided by its estimated standard deviation, refitting without the point. Leverage: the diagonal of the hat matrix, h = x'(X'X)⁻¹x, between 1/n and 1. Try dragging a point far to the right at an ordinary mpg (leverage jumps, line follows) and then straight up at an ordinary horsepower (studentized residual jumps, line barely moves).
