Growing and pruning a regression tree

The Hitters data from the slides: the log salary of 263 baseball players, predicted from the years they have played in the major leagues and the hits they made in the previous year. The tree is grown by recursive binary splitting until the nodes are small, then pruned back with cost complexity pruning. Moving α walks along the sequence of nested subtrees, and 6-fold cross-validation picks one of them.

  1. Start from the unpruned tree: many small boxes in the left panel, a deep tree in the middle one.
  2. Raise α. Each step collapses the weakest link, and the boxes merge back into larger ones.
  3. Compare the training, cross-validation and test errors on the right, and jump to the size cross-validation picks.

The tree is grown with stopping rules modelled on the defaults of R's tree() (a node is split only if it holds at least 10 players, each child keeps at least 5, and the node's deviance is at least 1% of the root's), and the unpruned tree on all 263 players has 29 leaves. Pruning it to 3 leaves gives exactly the tree of the slides: Years < 4.5 with a mean log salary of 5.107 (about 165 thousand dollars), then Hits < 117.5 with 5.998 and 6.740.

The training MSE always prefers the largest tree. Cross-validation does not, because a small box fits the noise of the few players inside it. The cross-validation curve is fairly flat near its minimum, so the size it picks moves with the random split and folds. The default seed lands on 3 leaves, as on the slides; other seeds pick anything from 3 to about 12, and what stays the same is that a handful of leaves does as well as twenty.
