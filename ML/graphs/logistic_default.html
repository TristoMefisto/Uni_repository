Logistic regression: probability of default on credit-card balance

ISLR Figure 4.2 and Tables 4.1 to 4.4, Default data (10 000 customers, 333 defaulted). A straight line fitted to a 0/1 outcome predicts negative probabilities; the logistic curve stays inside [0, 1]. The coefficients are found by maximum likelihood, here with Newton's method step by step, and a threshold turns the probabilities into a classifier.

  1. Grey line: a straight line fitted to 0/1, negative for small balances. Navy: the logistic curve, always between 0 and 1.
  2. Press Run Newton: the curve settles as the coefficients converge to the maximum-likelihood values.
  3. Move the threshold τ: the confusion matrix and the dot on the ROC curve follow.

A one-unit increase in balance raises the log odds of default by β̂₁ = 0.0055; a $1,000 increase multiplies the odds by e5.5 ≈ 245. The training error at τ = 0.5 is 2.75%, but the null classifier that never predicts default gets 3.33%: overall error is a poor yardstick when one class is rare, which is why sensitivity, specificity and the ROC curve exist.

Newton's method: start from β₁ = 0, and at each step move by −H⁻¹ g, where g is the gradient of the log-likelihood and H its Hessian. On standardised balance it converges in a handful of steps.
