Raise the cigarette excise: fewer smokers, fewer CVD admissions

A higher excise raises the price of a pack, and some smokers quit. Since smoking causes a known share of cardiovascular disease (CVD), fewer smokers means fewer CVD hospital admissions and lower costs for the SSN, while the State collects more tax. Move the excise and every number follows; the band shows the range given by the elasticity estimates.

How the numbers are built

Five steps, each with its formula, its inputs and its result for the excise you chose. All inputs are fixed at the values in the group's parameter sheet; only the excise moves.

1

From the excise to the pack price

VAT and the retailer's margin are shares of the price (22/122 and 10%), and the manufacturer keeps the same euros per pack. So each euro of extra excise raises the price by about €1.39, and 90% of the price rise goes to the State as excise plus VAT.

ΔP = ΔE / (1 − v − r)
T₁ = T₀ + 0.9 ΔP

  • v, rVAT (22/122 = 18.0%) and retailer's margin (10%), as shares of the price

3

CVD admissions caused by smoking, and those avoided

The population attributable fraction (PAF) is the share of CVD admissions that would not happen if no one smoked. When a share X of the population quits, their risk falls from the smokers' level to the level of people who quit less than five years ago, at once.

PAF = Pe(RRc − 1) / (1 + Pe(RRc − 1))
ΔH = H × X (RRc − RRf) / (1 + Pe(RRc − 1))
  • RRcrelative risk of current smokers against never smokers, 2.07
  • RRfrelative risk of people who quit less than 5 years ago, 1.74
  • HCVD hospital admissions a year, 600,436

Tax revenue

Revenue is packs sold times tax per pack. Packs sold fall with the price, but by less than the tax rises, so revenue goes up. It is a transfer from smokers to the State, kept apart from the health gains.

ΔR = Q₁T₁ − Q₀T₀

Limitations

The figures are a calibration from published parameters, not a forecast: useful for orders of magnitude and for seeing which input drives the result. This is not a cost-benefit analysis: smokers' and producers' welfare is not counted.

What is left out (so the benefit is understated)

  • Diseases other than CVD (lung cancer, COPD) and second-hand smoke.
  • Costs outside the hospital stay: drugs, outpatient care, rehabilitation; the €7,490 is a lower bound.
  • Young people who never start.

What may overstate it

  • Quitters reach the risk of people who quit five years ago at once; in reality their risk falls over several years.
  • Smokers who move to heated tobacco or e-cigarettes count as quitters.
  • Quitters may turn to other habits (food, alcohol).

Parameters

  • The relative risks are for CVD deaths among people aged 60+, applied here to admissions at all ages.
  • One elasticity for all smokers and all price levels; illicit and cross-border buying enter only through the range.
  • Packs sold respond with Kohler's elasticity, estimated on packs per smoker; counting the packs quitters stop buying (ε + εprev) would lower the revenue by about a quarter.

Policy

  • The rise must cover all substitutes: heated tobacco pays much less excise than cigarettes at the same price.
  • Cheap brands sit at the minimum tax, which must rise in step.
  • The tax is regressive: it weighs more on poorer smokers.
  • No indexation: inflation would slowly erode the rise.
