Health-benefit calculator · Italy · Current Public Policy Issues

Raise the cigarette excise on 1 January 2027

A higher specific excise raises the pack price, some smokers quit, and over the following years fewer people die of or are admitted to hospital for cardiovascular disease (CVD). The gains are counted as deaths, hospital admissions and life-years, plus the hospital money the SSN saves; the extra tax revenue is shown last, apart. The excise is not indexed, so inflation slowly erodes it. Every number comes from the inputs below, and every input has a source.

Year by year

Benefits arrive slowly: smoking falls in the first year, but a quitter's extra CVD risk fades over 5 to 15 years. Because the excise is not indexed, inflation slowly erodes the extra excise, and the price gap narrows a little each year.

From the excise to the real pack price

A pack's price is the specific excise S, plus three parts that are shares of the price (the ad valorem excise, VAT and the retailer's margin), plus the manufacturer's share. We assume both margins stay constant: the retailer keeps the 10% set by law, and the manufacturer's share per pack stays the same in real terms. Then only the excise moves the price, and each euro of specific excise adds about €4.4 at the till. Current law (L. 199/2025) raises S to €0.71 a pack in 2027 and €0.77 in 2028, then freezes it; the policy adds ΔS from 2027, also not indexed. Prices are in 2026 euros, so inflation slowly erodes both.

ΔP = θ m ΔS, m ≈ 4.4
S(policy) = S(law) + ΔS; real S = S / (1 + π)^(t − 2026)
real price = P₀ + m (real S − S₀)

From fewer smokers to CVD deaths and admissions avoided

Smoking causes a known part of CVD, its population attributable fraction (PAF). GBD puts the CVD deaths caused by smoking at 11,840 a year, about 6% of the 206,119 CVD deaths ISTAT counts for 2023; Possenti et al. put the CVD admissions at 177,409. The policy removes a share ρ of that burden: the part caused by current smokers (s), times the share of them who quit (f), times the share of the quitters' extra risk already gone (g). In epidemiology this is the potential impact fraction, PIF = PAF × ρ.

burden caused by smoking = PAF × all CVD
ρ = s f g
deaths avoided = ρ D̄; admissions avoided = ρ H̄

Life-years gained and SSN savings

The same share ρ applies to the other burdens smoking causes through CVD: the years of life lost and the SSN cost of the admissions. We approximate the years of life lost as about 10 per death (life expectancy at 75, where most CVD deaths happen, is 13.4 years); GBD's attributable years of life lost would replace this. Only the savings are in euros, discounted back to 2027. A euro value for the life-years is shown apart, because it rests on one contested number: the value of a life-year.

life-years gained = ρ × years of life lost to smoking
SSN savings = ρ × SSN cost caused by smoking

Tax revenue

Revenue is packs sold times tax per pack (specific excise, ad valorem excise and VAT), under the policy minus under current law. Packs sold fall for two reasons: fewer smokers (εp, step 2) and fewer packs per remaining smoker (εq). They still fall less than the tax rises, so revenue goes up. It is a transfer from smokers to the state, not a gain for society, so it is kept apart from the health gains; it is the number the MEF looks at.

tax per pack = S + (a + v) P
Q = Q₀ (real price / P₀)^(εp + εq)

Why 2027–2035

The default horizon is nine years. It covers the 2027 budget and the following ones of the next legislature, and ends with the EU Safe Hearts Plan target year (premature CVD mortality −25% by 2035 against 2022; COM(2025) 1024). By then about 90% of the health gain of the 2027 quitters has arrived. The 2027–29 option matches the three-year budget plan a finance minister signs; 2027–46 shows the long run.

Limitations

The figures are a calibrated projection built from published parameters, not a forecast. They are useful for orders of magnitude and for seeing which input drives the result.

What is left out (so the benefit is understated)

  • Cancers, lung disease and second-hand smoke: CVD is about 23% of the 52,601 deaths GBD attributes to smoking in Italy.
  • Non-fatal CVD apart from hospital costs (quality of life, informal care, lost work).
  • Young people who never start, whose gain comes after the horizon.

What may overstate it

  • One elasticity for every age, while most CVD deaths are at 75+, where evidence on how smokers respond is mixed.
  • Smokers who move to heated tobacco or e-cigarettes count as quitters; their CVD risk is lower than a smoker's, not zero.
  • Deaths are delayed, not prevented for ever: health costs in the added years are not subtracted.
  • λ = 0.25 is the age-50 rate; at the ages most CVD deaths happen, risk fades more slowly (λ = 0.15 gives about a quarter fewer deaths avoided).

Left out for now

  • Indexation of the excise to inflation (kept in the project notes as an option).
  • Smokers' lost surplus on the packs they give up, which would count against the benefit if smokers were fully rational.
  • A euro value for the lives saved, shown only apart: it moves by a factor of two with the value of a life-year (€40,000 to €89,800).

Prices and baseline

  • Margins are held constant: the retailer's 10% (set by law) and the manufacturer's share in real terms. If manufacturers absorb part of the tax, set θ below 1.
  • L. 199/2025 also trims the ad valorem rate (49.23% to 48% by 2028). Ignoring it puts the current-law price about 3–5% too high, but changes the deaths avoided by less than 1%.
  • Every pack is treated like the €5.50 reference pack. The minimum tax must rise with the excise, or cheap brands (much of the market sits near it) do not move.
  • Heated tobacco is left at current law; raising it in step avoids substitution.
  • Revenue counts excise and VAT on cigarettes only; VAT on the spending that moves to other goods is ignored.
  • Prevalence is flat apart from the price effect (it has been at about 20% for ten years); smoking-attributable CVD deaths are held at their 2023 level (they fell 2.2% a year in 2015–2023).
