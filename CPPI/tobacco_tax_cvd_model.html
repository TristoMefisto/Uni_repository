Raise the cigarette excise on 1 January 2027, then index it to inflation

A higher specific excise raises the pack price, some smokers quit, and over the following years fewer people die of or are admitted to hospital for cardiovascular disease (CVD). Indexing the excise stops the real price from falling again. Every number below comes from the inputs on this page, and every input has a source.

Year by year

Benefits arrive slowly: smoking falls in the first year, but a quitter's extra CVD risk fades over 5 to 15 years. With indexation the gap between the policy price and the current-law price also widens, because under current law the excise is frozen from 2028 and the real price drifts down.

From the excise to the pack price

The retail price is the specific excise S, the ad valorem excise aP, VAT vP, the retailer's margin rP and the manufacturer's share F. If manufacturers keep their share, one euro of specific excise adds about €4.4 at the till, because the ad valorem part, VAT and the retailer's margin all grow with the price. Current law (L. 199/2025) already sets S at €32.00, €35.50 and €38.50 per 1,000 for 2026, 2027 and 2028, and a at 49.23%, 48.5% and 48%; after 2028 nothing is indexed.

P = (S + F) / (1 − a − v − r) policy price = current-law price + θ × ΔS / (1 − a − v − r)

Indexation keeps the real price from falling

From 2028 the specific excise grows each year by k × inflation, on top of the steps already in current law. If k = 100% and manufacturers' share grows with inflation, the whole price grows with inflation and its real value stays constant. With k = 0 the real price falls by roughly half the inflation rate each year.

S(t) = S(t−1) × (1 + k × π) + current-law step(t) real price(t) = P(t) / (1 + π)^(t − 2026)

From fewer smokers to fewer CVD deaths and admissions

Smoking causes a known number of CVD deaths and hospital admissions each year. The share of smokers who quit (step 3) removes the same share of the part caused by current smokers, but not at once: a quitter's extra risk fades over the years, so only a share g(t) of the gain has arrived by year t.

Tax revenue and smokers' lost surplus

Revenue is packs sold times tax per pack. Demand is inelastic, so revenue rises. Smokers who buy less lose the surplus on the packs they give up: a small triangle under the demand curve.

Adding up

Revenue is a transfer from smokers to the state, so it is shown but not added. The rest depends on how smokers are seen. If smoking is partly a mistake, quitters' health gains are a benefit to society. If smokers are fully rational, they already weighed their own health, and only the SSN savings count against their lost surplus. Future years are discounted.

not fully rational: net benefit(t) = value of health(t) + SSN savings(t) fully rational: net benefit(t) = SSN savings(t) − lost surplus(t) present value = Σ net benefit(t) / (1 + d)^(t − 2027)

Why 2027–2035

The default horizon is nine years. It covers the 2027 budget and the following ones of the next legislature, and ends with the EU Safe Hearts Plan target year (premature CVD mortality −25% by 2035 against 2022). By then about 90% of the health gain of the 2027 quitters has arrived. The 2027–29 option matches the three-year budget plan a finance minister signs; 2027–46 shows the long run.

Prices and taxes

P = (S + F) / (1 − a − v − r) F(2026) = P₀ (1 − a − v − r) − S(2026), F(t) = F(2026) (1 + π)^(t−2026) ΔP = θ ΔS / (1 − a − v − r) ≈ 4.4 θ ΔS tax per pack T = S + (a + v) P minimum tax raised in step: ΔM = (1 − r) ΔP

Smokers and sales

N(t) = N₀ (P̃(t)/P₀)^εp, P̃ = real price f(t) = 1 − (P̃_policy(t) / P̃_law(t))^εp Q(t) = Q₀ (P̃(t)/P₀)^εQ, packs per smoker q̄ = Q₀/N₀ revenue R = Q × T = N × q̄ × T

Health

g(t) = 1 − e^(−λ (t − 2026)) deaths avoided D(t) = A × s × f(t) × g(t) admissions avoided H(t) = H × s × f(t) × g(t) value of health V(t) = D(t) × value per death (≈ 10 × €89,800) SSN savings C(t) = H(t) × cost per admission

Welfare

lost surplus L(t) = ½ (P̃_policy − P̃_law)(Q_law − Q_policy) net benefit = V + C (not fully rational) net benefit = C − L (fully rational) revenue = transfer, shown apart PV = Σ net benefit(t) / (1 + d)^(t−2027)

Limitations

The figures are a calibrated projection built from published parameters, not a forecast. They are useful for orders of magnitude and for seeing which input drives the result.

What is left out (so the benefit is understated)

  • Cancers, lung disease and second-hand smoke: CVD is about 23% of the 52,601 deaths GBD attributes to smoking in Italy.
  • Non-fatal CVD apart from hospital costs (quality of life, informal care, lost work).
  • Young people who never start, whose gain comes after the horizon.

What may overstate it

  • One elasticity for every age, while most CVD deaths are at 75+, where evidence on how smokers respond is mixed.
  • Smokers who move to heated tobacco or e-cigarettes count as quitters; their CVD risk is lower than a smoker's, not zero.
  • Deaths are delayed, not prevented for ever: health costs in the added years are not subtracted.
  • The phase-in treats every quitter as if they quit in 2027, which slightly flatters the later quitters that indexation adds.

Taxes and prices

  • Every pack is treated like the €5.50 reference pack. The minimum tax must rise with the excise, or cheap brands (much of the market sits near it) do not move.
  • Heated tobacco is left at current law; raising it in step avoids substitution.
  • Revenue counts excise and VAT on cigarettes only; VAT on the spending that shifts to other goods is ignored.
  • Smokers' lost surplus uses legal sales, so packs that move to illicit buying count as given up (this overstates the cost in the fully rational view).

Baseline

  • Prevalence is flat apart from the price effect (it has been at about 20% for ten years); smoking-attributable CVD deaths are held at their 2023 level (they fell 2.2% a year in 2015–2023).
  • Producers' and retailers' margins on packs no longer sold are not counted (much of the industry is foreign-owned).
