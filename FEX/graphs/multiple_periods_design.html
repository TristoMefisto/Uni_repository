More T in experiments: allocating a fixed survey budget

McKenzie (2012). The budget pays for K surveys in total, split between units and rounds: K = 2n(m + r), with m pre-treatment rounds, r post-treatment rounds and n units in each group. The standard error of the ATE has a closed form for each of the three estimators, so the best allocation can be read off directly, and it depends on the autocorrelation.

  1. Set the autocorrelation first. It decides everything else: with ρ high the baseline rounds are worth paying for, with ρ low they are not.
  2. Move m and r. The number of units n falls automatically, because the budget is fixed.
  3. The grid at the bottom is every allocation the budget allows. The cross marks the best one for the estimator you picked, the ring marks the allocation you are on.

Two regularities come out of the grid. When ρ is high the minimum sits at a small number of rounds, because repeated observations of the same unit carry nearly the same information and units are worth more than rounds, but one baseline round is worth having (the same intuition as clustered randomization). When ρ is low the baseline predicts nothing, DD is the worst of the three, and the money is better spent on follow-ups that average out noise, or on units if the estimator is D.
