Randomization inference: the p-value from placebo assignments

Only one assignment was run, so only one potential outcome per unit is observed. Under the sharp null (no effect for any unit) the missing column is just a copy of the observed one, and every other assignment the lottery could have produced becomes a placebo experiment. The RI p-value is the share of placebos with an estimate at least as extreme as the real one.

  1. One column per unit was observed. The sharp null says the treatment did nothing, so the missing column is a copy of the observed one (purple).
  2. Press One placebo: re-run the lottery on those numbers and recompute the difference in means.
  3. Press Run: all placebos. The p-value is the share of them at least as extreme as the real estimate (red).

The t-test it replaces (weak null)

The sharp null is stronger than the weak null of the t-test: no unit at all is affected, not just the average. That is what makes the placebo outcomes computable. Drag τ up to add a real effect to the treated units and watch the observed estimate move into the tail of the placebo distribution.
