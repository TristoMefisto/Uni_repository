Power, the MDE and the sample size

A power calculation is a sample size calculation: it guesses the standard error of the treatment effect before the experiment is run. The numbers here start from the L5 example, out-of-pocket health expenditures at baseline in evaluation.dta (5,628 eligible households, mean 14.53, SD 4.30), and the design parameters are the ones the lecture adds one at a time.

  1. Move N and watch the two distributions pull apart. Power is the shaded area of the right one beyond the critical value.
  2. The middle panel is the same thing as a curve. The horizontal line is the target power, the vertical dashed line the N it requires.
  3. Turn on a complication (ICC, partial compliance, blocking) and see the required N move in the right panel.

Everything on this page is the closed form, not a simulation. The quantiles are normal ones, which is what sampsi uses (power twomeans uses the t distribution and solves the degrees of freedom iteratively, so it returns slightly larger samples). At the defaults the required N is 291 per group, the same number the slides report.

The complications are the multiplicative terms of the lecture. Clustering enters as the design effect √(1 + (ng − 1)ρu), which is 1 when the cluster holds one unit or when the ICC is 0, and grows with both. Blocking enters as √(1 − R2B) and is the only term that helps. Noncompliance dilutes the effect the experiment actually delivers, so the signal becomes β1(c − s) and the required sample grows with 1/(c − s)2: halving take-up multiplies the sample by 4.

With clustering on, the critical values should use G − 2 degrees of freedom rather than the normal approximation, so the required N here is mildly optimistic when the number of clusters is small.
