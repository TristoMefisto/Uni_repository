Interference: job search assistance with fixed vacancies

The numerical example of L7. There are NJ job seekers and NV vacancies, so without the program everyone finds a job with probability φ = NV/NJ. A share π of them gets assistance and finds a job for sure; the others share the vacancies that are left. The program creates no job. All the numbers are expectations in the model, so there is no sampling noise.

  1. Start from the slide's numbers (φ = 0.10, π = 0.01): the NATE and the TATE almost coincide and the IATE is tiny.
  2. Push π towards φ: the IATE grows in absolute value and the NATE moves away from the TATE.
  3. Watch the implementation effect: whatever π and φ, the jobs gained by the assisted are exactly the jobs lost by the others, so the ImE stays at 0.

With the slide's numbers the TATE is 0.9, the IATE −0.009 and the NATE 0.909, so a naive evaluator would hardly notice the interference. Yet the ImE is 0.01 × 0.909 − 0.009 ≈ 0, and in this model it is exactly 0 for every π and φ: the number of jobs is fixed at NV, so the employment rate of the treated area stays at φ. Every job an assisted job seeker gets is a job a non-assisted one does not get. The IATE is small only because it is spread over many people, and its weight in the ImE (1, against π for the NATE) is what makes it matter.
