Clustered random assignment: same individuals, fewer coin flips

Individuals sit in clusters (schools, villages, branches). Assigning the treatment cluster by cluster is often the only feasible design, but the outcomes are still measured per individual. How much precision is lost depends on how similar the members of a cluster are to each other: the intra-cluster correlation ρ.

  1. Boxes are clusters, dots are people. Raise ρ to make the people inside a box alike.
  2. Press Run: top histogram assigns treatment person by person, bottom one cluster by cluster. Same people, same ATE.
  3. The ratio of the two SDs is the price of clustering. The right panel compares it with the textbook formula.

Potential outcomes are Y(0) = 50 + cj + eij with a cluster effect cj of variance 100ρ and an individual part of variance 100(1−ρ), and Y(1) = Y(0) + 5. The ICC formula and the design effect are ahead of the notes (L4); here they only label what the histograms already show.

With the unequal-sizes box ticked, cluster sizes range from small to large and bigger clusters have higher outcomes. The pooled difference in means then over-weights whichever large clusters happen to be treated: its average over assignments drifts from the ATE. The bias fades as G grows.
