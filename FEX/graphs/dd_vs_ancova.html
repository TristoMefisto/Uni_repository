D, DD and ANCOVA: which estimator is more precise

One baseline round and one follow-up round, treatment randomly assigned. All three estimators are unbiased for the ATE, so the whole question is the width of their sampling distributions, and that is set by the autocorrelation of the outcome between the two rounds.

  1. Set the autocorrelation with the slider and watch the three sampling distributions on the right change width while they stay centred on the true ATE.
  2. Cross ρ = 0.5: below it DD is wider than the plain difference in means, above it DD is narrower. ANCOVA is never worse than either.
  3. Read the ratio curve: at ρ = 0 DD needs twice the sample size of ANCOVA for the same precision, at ρ = 1 they are the same.

The data generating process is the one behind Proposition 3 and Proposition 4: the same unit is measured twice, the two measurements share a variance σ² and a correlation ρ, and the treatment shifts the second one by the ATE. DD subtracts the baseline with a coefficient fixed at 1, ANCOVA estimates that coefficient, which is why ANCOVA is never the worse of the two and is strictly better whenever ρ is below 1. The plain difference in means throws the baseline away, so it is beaten by ANCOVA for any ρ above 0, and by DD only once ρ passes 0.5.
