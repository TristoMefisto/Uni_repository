Random effects, fixed effects and the Hausman test

Random effects is OLS on partially demeaned data: a share θ of each unit's mean is removed, with θ between 0 (pooled OLS) and 1 (fixed effects). The first tab follows the coefficients of the slides' union-premium example (WAGEPAN, 545 men, 1980-1987, slides 603-613) as θ moves from 0 to 1. The second tab runs the modern Hausman test, the correlated random effects regression of slides 619-630, on AIRFARE (1,149 routes, 1997-2000). Everything is estimated in the page and matches the slides' Stata output.

  1. Move θ. At 0 the union premium is .175 (POLS); at the estimated θ̂ = .643 it is .108 (RE); at 1 it is .083 (FE). The more of the between-person variation we remove, the smaller it gets.
  2. Education is constant over time for every man: its column is multiplied by (1 − θ), so its interval explodes as θ → 1 and FE cannot estimate it at all.
  3. The second panel shows where θ̂ comes from: it grows with the number of periods T and with σₐ²/σᵤ², the share of the error that is a permanent person effect.
  1. The first panel compares routes with each other (route averages): more concentrated routes are more expensive. The second compares each route with itself over time: the slope is smaller.
  2. RE mixes the two and gives .209; FE uses only the second and gives .169. Are they different because of chance, or because the route effect is correlated with concentration?
  3. The CRE regression answers it: add the route average of concen to the RE model. Its coefficient is significant (t = 2.62), so RE is rejected in favour of FE, and the coefficient on concen itself becomes exactly the FE estimate.

The test

RE and FE use the same model; they differ in the assumption about aᵢ. RE assumes it is uncorrelated with every xᵢₜ, which lets it keep a share 1 − θ of the between variation and so estimate time-constant variables like education, and be more precise. FE allows any correlation and pays by discarding the between variation. In WAGEPAN θ̂ = .643 sits in the middle, and the union and marriage premiums drop from RE to FE (.108 to .083, .077 to .058): men who join unions or marry are different to begin with.

The Hausman logic is to compare the two: if RE's assumption holds, both are consistent and should be close. The CRE version on slides 619-630 makes the comparison a regression test, robust to clustering: add the person (route) averages of the time-varying regressors to the RE model. If their coefficients are zero the averages carry no information about aᵢ and RE is fine. In AIRFARE the t statistic on concenbar is 2.62, so RE is rejected and FE (.169) is the estimate to report. Route distance, constant over time, can be kept in the CRE model, which is an advantage over plain FE.
