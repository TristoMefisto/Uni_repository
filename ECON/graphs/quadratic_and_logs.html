Quadratics and logarithms

Two functional forms from lecture 06, both closed form, with the slides' estimates as presets (rounded as the slides round them, so the turning points match slides 403 and 460). A quadratic in x makes the effect of x change with x and gives a turning point. A log makes coefficients read as percentages, but 100·β is an approximation that gets worse as β grows.

  1. Pick a preset. The curve is the part of ŷ that depends on x, with the turning point x* = |β̂₁/(2β̂₂)| marked.
  2. Move x₀: the tangent and the second panel give the slope β̂₁ + 2β̂₂x₀ there. In the slides' wage example it falls from 12.3% for the first year to 4.7% at ten years.
  3. Try the four sign cases: only when β̂₁ and β̂₂ have opposite signs is the turning point at a positive x.
  1. Move β̂ and watch 100·β̂ (dashed) and the exact 100·(eβ̂ − 1) separate as |β̂| grows.
  2. The presets are the slides' coefficients: one more room (.306) is worth 35.8%, not 30.6%.
  3. The second panel is the other direction: a log difference is close to the percentage change only for small changes (unem from 8 to 9 is 12.5%, the log difference .118).

In a quadratic the coefficient on x is not "the effect of x": it is the slope at x = 0. The slides' wage equation (WAGE2, 759 men) has lwage = −.192 + .136 educ + .123 exper − .0038 exper², so the first year of experience is worth about 12.3%, the eleventh about 4.7% by the derivative (4.3% exactly), and the return turns negative after 16.2 years. Slide 403 contrasts it with the textbook's 24.4 years, which is the WAGE1 equation for the wage level without education (wage = 3.73 + .298 exper − .0061 exper²), available as a preset. With a turning point near the edge of the data, "the effect becomes negative" may say more about the functional form than about workers.

For logs, 100·β̂ is fine for coefficients up to about .1 in absolute value. Beyond that, report 100·(eβ̂ − 1): the female coefficient −.2202 of slide 381 is a gap of 19.8%, not 22%, and the married men coefficient .213 of slide 429 is 23.7%, not 21.3%.
